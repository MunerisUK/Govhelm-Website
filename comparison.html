How GovHelm compares

An honest comparison is worth more to you than a flattering one, so this page starts with what GovHelm has not done. Every capability claim on this site says whether it is deployed, built or only designed.

What is actually builtWhere the alternatives win

How to read this page

Three words, used precisely

Software comparisons usually collapse a roadmap and a running service into the same tick. These three markers are used the same way everywhere on this site so that they cannot be confused.

Deployed

Running in a live government service, handling real citizens.

Built

Code complete in a released version. You can run it today and test it.

Designed

Registered, specified and scheduled — not yet wired.

GovHelm has no production deployments

This is the most important line on the page, so it is not buried in a footnote. GovHelm is pre-release. The platform runs, the tests pass and the reference profiles load, but no government is running a live citizen service on it.

Every established supplier can say the opposite, and that is a real advantage they hold over us. If proven production scale at national volume is your primary criterion today, you should weight it accordingly, and we will not argue.

Where the alternatives win

Four things we will not argue with

If any of these is your binding constraint, the honest answer is that something else fits better today. We would rather say so now than in month four of an evaluation.

Proven scale, today

An established platform has run national-volume services for years. We have run tests. If that is the criterion that decides it, it decides it.

A supplier ecosystem

Large platforms come with a market of implementers, trained staff and a second source. A young product comes with the people who built it, and that is a genuine concentration risk.

Breadth of packaged function

A suite that has been sold for twenty years has more in it. Much of it you will not use, but where you need a deep vertical module today, it may already exist there and not here.

Procurement familiarity

A framework agreement your procurement team has used before is faster to buy through than a new supplier, whatever the merits. That is a real cost of choosing us.

Where we win

And four things they will not argue with

The tenth government costs a fraction of the first. One image, no fork, and a capability built once is available to every tenant behind a toggle. No competitor whose model is per-government configuration or bespoke build can match that structurally, however they price it.

Governance is not an add-on. Minimisation, purpose-bound agreements and a citizen-readable audit trail are how a read happens at all. Elsewhere they are a workstream, and workstreams get cut when a programme is late.

Sovereignty is a deployment property, not a contractual assurance. One government, one deployment, one region, one database, its own keys — and cross-tenant access forbidden in the profile rather than filtered in a query.

We do not need to replace your systems of record. The proposal that keeps your ERP, your HCM and your analytics estate is both the cheapest and the lowest-risk one on the table, and it is the only one we make.

Questions worth asking every supplier

  • Show me the log of who read a citizen’s record last month, and what was refused
  • Which of these capability claims is running in a live service today?
  • What exactly changes when the next jurisdiction is onboarded?
  • Where does the data physically sit, and who can compel its disclosure?
  • What does it cost to change the allocation rules in year three?
  • What do I get on exit, in what format, and is it in the contract?

We would rather you asked us these than took this page on trust. Two of them are questions we answer better than we answer the rest.

Platform

Security & data

A platform holding a whole government’s citizen record has to answer for itself before it asks for anything. This page sets out what is enforced, where it is enforced, and what has not been independently assured yet.

Request the security packArchitecture

Minimisation is the default, not a setting

A cross-domain consumer receives a derived claim computed by the owning domain — whether a figure is below a threshold, whether an employment is active — and never the underlying value. The domain that owns a fact sees its own data; nobody else does.

This is enforced by absence as much as by rules. Raw income appears in no agreement, so there is no consumer, no purpose and no configuration under which it is returned. A future service cannot be granted it by an administrator ticking a box.

Purpose-bound agreements

Every access is evaluated against an agreement naming the consumer, the domain, the purpose and the exact permitted fields, with its legal basis recorded. The same claim read by two services for two purposes needs two agreements: trusted once is not trusted always.

A jurisdiction declares its own register in its profile or inherits the platform baseline, because data-protection regimes differ by country. Either way the register is version-controlled configuration, reviewable in a pull request.

The citizen can read the log

Allow and deny are both written to an immutable per-tenant audit log, and the citizen has a dashboard onto it: who accessed their data, under which agreement, for what purpose — and what was refused. Refusals are the entries an oversight body most wants and most systems discard.

Identity resolutions are logged on the same footing. Looking a person up is an access, whether or not it matched.

Identifiers are aliases; the key is not

The internal key that joins domains is opaque and is never one of the issued numbers. A leaked passport number, social security number or registration card number unlocks nothing, because none of them is the join. A scheme a jurisdiction has not declared is refused rather than resolved on a best guess.

Isolation is structural

Tenancy here is not a column in a query that somebody must remember to filter. One government per deployment, one database per government, one region per government, and cross-tenant access forbidden in every profile — asserted by a test across every committed tenant.

Around the pod, a default-deny network posture: ingress only from the ingress controller, nothing else permitted by default.

Sovereignty by design

Each government’s deployment is placed in its own declared region, with customer-managed keys, its own database and its own host. The residency block is part of the profile schema rather than an operational afterthought, because for a jurisdiction choosing between suppliers, “where does the data physically live, and who can be compelled to hand it over” is frequently the question that decides the procurement.

The rules are public; the data never is

Per tenant, the agreement register and the capability catalogue are published as open data in DCAT JSON-LD with plain-JSON distributions. Anyone can audit the rules that gate access to citizen data without seeing any citizen data. Nothing personal is in that layer by construction — the minimisation guarantee that keeps the record closed is exactly what lets the metadata be open.

Limits

What this page does not claim

A security page that only lists strengths is a marketing page. These are the gaps as they stand today.

No independent assurance yet

The minimisation and audit design has not been independently assured. For a platform whose entire proposition is trust, that is the most important item on the roadmap, and it is on it.

No production deployment

Nothing here has been tested by a live citizen service at scale. Pre-release means pre-release, and the comparison page says so before it says anything else.

Live transport is a deployment concern

The connector seam is built and demonstrable; the live wire to a counterparty system is configured per deployment and is not exercised by the reference build.

Penetration testing is scheduled, not done

An external test against a reference deployment is planned ahead of the first production tenancy. Until it has happened, we will not imply that it has.

Reporting a vulnerability

Security reports are handled ahead of anything else. The route in is on the contact page; a coordinated disclosure policy is published alongside the first production deployment.

Platform

What the platform does

Eleven things, each of which exists because a principle needed somewhere to live. The four marked as wired are running in the reference build today; you can drive them in an evaluation environment.

Book a demonstrationSee the architecture

Record · Built

Single citizen record

A thin master of identity attributes plus pointers to the systems that own the detail. Collect once, never duplicate — and nothing is copied into the middle to make a service work.

Governance · Built

Policy-as-code decision point

Every cross-domain read is evaluated against an active data-sharing agreement naming consumer, domain, purpose and the exact permitted fields. No agreement, no access.

Governance · Built

Citizen-visible audit trail

Allow and deny are both written to a log the citizen can read: who accessed their data, under which agreement, for what purpose, and what was refused.

Identity · Built

Identity resolver

A citizen holds many identifiers from many issuing authorities. The resolver maps any accepted one to a single opaque internal key — which is never one of the issued numbers.

Events · Built

Event backbone

An authoritative write publishes a fact carrying a reference. Subscribers read the one record and act. The producer never learns who consumes it.

Services · Built

Composed services

Benefits runs on the identity master and the same revenue claim Identity reads, as a different consumer under a different purpose, with no store of its own.

Integration · Built

Governed connector seam

Health, analytics, finance and HR are external systems of record brokered across one seam. Claims in, instructions out, every field policy-gated and audited.

Transparency · Built

Open-data catalogue

The agreement register and capability catalogue are published per tenant as DCAT JSON-LD, with plain-JSON distributions. Rules public; data never.

Tenancy · Built

Profile-driven multi-tenancy

Every jurisdictional difference is a validated value in a profile. A missing variation point is a startup error, never a silent default.

Isolation · Built

Structural tenant isolation

One government per deployment, one database per government, cross-tenant access forbidden in every profile, and a default-deny network posture around the pod.

Assurance · Built

Live architecture view

An authenticated admin view serves the profile schema, the capability registry, the decision record and the risk register from the running system, not from a document.

Front end · Built

Server-rendered portal

Each government’s portal is rendered from its profile on the server: its logo, its languages, its currency, its enabled services. No client framework, so the service works without JavaScript.

Feature in depth

The record is thin on purpose

The temptation in every citizen-data programme is to build the warehouse: pull everything into one place so that it is easy to query. It is easy to query, and it is also a single catastrophic breach, a permanent reconciliation problem, and a legal basis nobody can articulate for half the fields.

GovHelm holds the master thin. Minimal identity attributes, a lifecycle, and pointers to the systems that own everything else. Revenue owns income. The care system owns clinical data. The finance system owns the ledger. When a service needs one of those facts, it reads it through the gate, by reference, for a stated purpose.

The practical consequence is that the interesting question stops being “where is the data?” and becomes “who is allowed to see what, and why?” — which is the question a regulator actually asks.

What the master holds

  • Legal name and the identity attributes that make a person findable
  • Lifecycle: alive, deceased, and the events that changed it
  • An opaque internal key that is never an issued identifier
  • Pointers to the authoritative source for each domain

What it never holds

  • Raw income — revenue owns the figure and no agreement exposes it
  • Clinical records — the care system owns them, and is reached by integration
  • Salary, pay band or bank details — payroll owns them
  • A copy of anything another system is authoritative for

An agreement, as the engine reads it

data_sharing_agreements[2] — from a jurisdiction profile

- id: AGR-BEN-REV
  consumer: benefits_service
  domain:   revenue.status
  purpose:  benefit_assessment
  fields:   [belowThreshold]     # a claim, not the figure
  legal_basis: public_task

The same revenue claim is read by the citizen portal under AGR-IDV-1 for a different purpose. Access is purpose-bound: trusted once is not trusted always.

Feature in depth

The agreement is the code path, not a document about it

In most systems a data-sharing agreement is a PDF in a governance folder and an access-control list somewhere else, and the relationship between the two is somebody’s memory. Here the agreement is the control. The decision point evaluates consumer, domain, purpose and requested fields against the tenant’s active register, and a field that is not listed is not returned.

A jurisdiction declares its own register in its profile, or inherits the platform baseline. Either way it is configuration under version control: you can see what was permitted last year, who changed it, and when — and the same register is published as open data so that anyone can check it.

Feature in depth

Looking someone up is an access, and is audited as one

A long-term resident’s strongest local credential may be a registration card while their travel document is issued by another state entirely. Real identity is a set of aliases, held by different authorities, changing over time — not one number.

The resolver takes any identifier a jurisdiction accepts and returns the one opaque internal key that joins domains. The key is never an issued number, so a leaked passport number unlocks nothing. A scheme the profile does not accept is refused outright, and every attempt — resolved or not, permitted or not — is written to the audit log, because a lookup is an access.

It was the first module written in TypeScript, so the identifier and decision shapes are checked at compile time: the minimisation contract is enforced by the type system as well as by the tests.

Schemes a profile can accept

  • A strong local seed, such as a social security number
  • A local anchor that works across nationalities, such as a registration card
  • A passport from any issuing state
  • A national identity card from any issuing state
  • A local driving licence

Issuers are ISO 3166-1 codes, or the sentinel ANY. Which schemes a tenant accepts is a profile decision, and a scheme it does not list is denied rather than best-guessed.

Feature in depth

Governed integration, in both directions

Health, analytics, finance and payroll all cross the same seam, with the same guarantees. Only the target and the field projection differ — which is why a new connector is a connector, not a project.

Health & care · Built

CommVita

Health and care is not implemented natively. A tenant that runs the capability configures a CommVita connector in its profile and the platform hands off, or pulls a minimal care summary, across the seam.

Connector detail

Analytics · Built

Microsoft Fabric / OneLake

Publishes governed, minimised data products into the tenant’s own analytics estate. A data product is not a raw dump: every field is gated by the policy engine under a declared agreement and written to the audit log, so with no agreement nothing is exported at all.

Connector detail

SAP, Oracle Fusion, Unit4, TechnologyOne · Built

Finance / ERP

Reads minimised claims about a citizen’s finance account — below the means threshold, tax compliant — never the raw figures, and accepts outbound payment or posting instructions for the finance system to action.

Connector detail

Workday, SuccessFactors, Oracle HCM, Zellis · Built

HR / Payroll

Government is also a large employer. The connector returns employment claims — is a government employee, employment active — never salary or pay band, and forwards a leaver instruction when a death is registered.

Connector detail

Drive it yourself

An evaluation environment loads your profile, your agreements and your capability toggles. Register a death, file an income, watch the entitlement recompute, then read the audit log as the citizen would.

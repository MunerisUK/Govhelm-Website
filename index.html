Sovereign · Governed by default · Config, not code

Joined-up government, without the ten-year programme

GovHelm is the governed integration layer that turns the systems a government already runs into one joined-up government. One citizen record, an enforced data-sharing agreement behind every read, and a new jurisdiction onboarded as a profile rather than a fork.

The problem

Government holds the same facts about you in dozens of places, and none of them agree

Name, address, date of birth, income, household, contact details — each service holds its own copy, each copy drifts, and every copy is another place a breach can start. The citizen pays for it by telling the same story to every department.

The usual answer is a multi-year systems-integration programme that replaces everything at once. Small and mid-sized governments cannot afford that and, more to the point, cannot de-risk it. The public record of those programmes is not encouraging, and a jurisdiction of a hundred thousand people does not get to write off a decade.

GovHelm takes the opposite position. Keep the registries, the finance system and the analytics estate you already run. Put one governed layer over them that holds the citizen record thin, resolves who is who, and checks every cross-domain read against an agreement a lawyer wrote and a machine enforces.

What changes first

  • A citizen tells government a fact once, and every service reads it
  • Every cross-domain read names its purpose and its legal basis
  • A death registered once ceases the pension and clears the electoral roll
  • An income change recomputes entitlement without anyone filling in a form
  • The citizen can read the log of who looked at their data, and why
  • Onboarding the next government is a profile, not a project

Every claim on this page is something you can put to the test in an evaluation environment, against your own profile, before you commit to anything.

How it works

A closed record, an open edge

  1. 1

    Hold the record once, and thin

    The master carries minimal identity attributes and pointers to the systems that own the detail. Income lives in revenue; clinical data lives in the care system. Nothing is copied into the middle to make a service work.

  2. 2

    Make every agreement executable

    A data-sharing agreement names the consumer, the domain, the purpose and the exact fields permitted. If a read is not covered by an active agreement, it does not happen — and the refusal is logged too.

  3. 3

    Return a claim, not the figure

    A benefits assessment needs to know whether someone is below a threshold, not what they earn. So that is what it gets: a derived yes or no. Raw income is in no agreement at all, so it is denied to everyone but revenue.

  4. 4

    Publish the fact, not the payload

    An authoritative write emits an event carrying a reference. Subscribers read the one record and act. The producer never knows who consumes it, and no figure travels on the wire.

Why this and not the alternatives

Five things that are structural, not marketing

Each of these is a property of how the platform is built. Each is testable in an evaluation environment, and the page that explains it says where the limits are.

Integrate, do not replace

The platform sits on top of the registries, finance, HR and analytics systems a government already runs. Joined-up services arrive in months while the systems of record stay where they are.

The integration seam

Governance is in the code path

Minimisation, purpose-bound agreements and a citizen-visible audit trail are how a read happens at all — not a policy document, and not a consulting deliverable bolted on at the end.

Security and data

Zero-customisation multi-tenancy

One image serves every government. A new jurisdiction is a profile and its connectors. There is no fork to maintain, so the tenth government costs a fraction of the first.

The profile model

Sovereignty by design

One isolated deployment per government, its own database, its own region, customer-managed keys, and cross-tenant access forbidden in every profile rather than filtered in a query.

Deployment topology

The rules are open data

The agreement register and the capability catalogue are published per tenant as DCAT, so anyone can audit the rules that gate access to citizen data without seeing any citizen data.

Open data endpoints

Priced against the population, not the seat

Per-tenant subscription banded by population, per-capability modules, and implementation quoted openly. Seat pricing was never designed for population-scale citizen services.

Commercial questions

The proof

Tell Us Once is not a slogan here, it is a test

One authoritative write fans out by reference to every subscribed service. Each fan-out is policy-checked on its own terms, and both the allows and the denies land in the citizen’s audit trail.

The same pattern carries the quieter, more valuable case. When a citizen files income, revenue — which owns the figure — publishes an event carrying a reference. Benefits is subscribed, re-reads the derived claim, and re-assesses entitlement. The citizen never opened the benefits page and never filled in a form.

That is what proactive government looks like when it is built rather than promised: update one fact once, and entitlements recompute. It falls out of the architecture instead of being a feature in a backlog.

What the platform does

Who it is for

Governments that need whole-of-government, at a scale the market ignores

The platform was designed around jurisdictions between thirty thousand and a few million people: whole-of-government needs, sovereignty that matters, and no appetite for a programme that outlives the administration that commissioned it.

Crown Dependencies

Jersey, Guernsey and the Isle of Man: consolidated departments, hard transformation budgets, and constitutional distinctiveness that a generic product cannot express and a bespoke build charges for.

Overseas Territories and small states

Gibraltar, Cayman, Malta, Barbados. Different currencies, different heads of state, different data-protection regimes, different tax bases — all of which the profile schema already carries as values.

Larger Commonwealth realms

The Jamaica profile is two orders of magnitude larger than the Crown Dependencies and runs on the same core and the same schema. Scale changes the sizing, not the architecture.

Commercial, and deliberately predictable

Priced per government, not per seat

GovHelm is commercial software. The subscription is per tenant, banded by population, with capability modules and a transparent implementation quote — because per-seat pricing was designed for enterprises, not for services every resident uses.

The hard commercial truth of government software is that the licence is rarely the largest line. The integration work is, and the change requests over the following five years are. A platform that is cheap to buy and expensive to change is not cheap.

So the things a jurisdiction changes most often — its agreements, its capability toggles, its branding, its constitutional facts — are profile configuration you control, reviewable in a pull request, not chargeable work.

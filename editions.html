Editions

Some domains need more depth than a whole-of-government capability tile can carry. Those become editions: products in their own right, on the same architecture, the same brand system and the same guarantees.

Ask about GovHelm HousingThe capability catalogue

The model

An edition is not a fork, and not a separate identity

A capability in the Common Core answers the whole-of-government question: a citizen can apply for housing and manage a tenancy, and the record behind it composes with everything else. That is the right depth for a government portal and the wrong depth for the team that runs an allocation scheme, a choice-based lettings cycle and a statutory homelessness duty.

So an edition takes one domain to the depth that domain needs, while inheriting the architecture: one record, one permission model, one audit trail, and a documented boundary to everything else. It is an edition of GovHelm, not a product that happens to share a logo.

What an edition inherits

  • The GovHelm mark, wordmark and typefaces
  • The architecture: one record, one gate, one audit trail
  • The accessibility and data-protection commitments
  • The commercial posture: licensed, low cost, no per-module upsell

What an edition owns

  • Its descriptor — the word after GovHelm
  • One accent colour, and a product icon
  • Its own domain model, at the depth that domain needs
  • Its own release train and its own roadmap

GovHelm Housing

Integrated housing management for UK local authorities and housing associations: the housing register, allocations, lettings, homelessness casework and tenancy management, in one commercially licensed, low-cost system.

  • Twelve interoperable modules on one household record
  • The allocation scheme written down as configurable rules
  • Homelessness duties and their statutory clocks in the case model
  • Statutory returns assembled from live case data

GovHelm Housing has its own site, which is not yet published. Until it is, the fastest route to a housing demonstration is the contact page.

The first edition

Housing, at the depth housing teams need

Housing was the obvious first edition because the gap is so wide. The whole-of-government capability says a citizen can apply for housing and manage a tenancy. A housing options team needs banding criteria, local lettings plans, advert cycles, bidding, prevention and relief duties, personalised housing plans, and returns that reconcile.

The edition carries all of that. It also carries the brand rules an edition has to obey: it uses the GovHelm mark and wordmark unchanged, owns only the Housing descriptor and a community-green accent, and keeps the platform blue for platform-level affordances so that the two never do the same job.

Like the platform, it is pre-release and says so. Its site marks every capability Deployed, Built or Designed, and nothing on it is marked Deployed either.

The rule

An edition may deepen a domain; it may not weaken a guarantee

The same invariants apply. An edition that wanted raw data where the platform returns a claim, or an audit trail the citizen could not read, would not be an edition.

Same governance

Minimisation, purpose-bound agreements and a citizen-visible audit trail, unchanged. Depth in a domain is never a reason to loosen a control.

Same architecture

One record, one permission model, one event pattern, a documented boundary to the finance and asset systems that stay where they are.

Same accessibility bar

WCAG 2.2 AA, server-rendered, working without JavaScript. An edition inherits the commitment along with the components.
